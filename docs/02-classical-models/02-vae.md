# Variational Autoencoders

> **Summary** — A VAE learns an encoder that maps data to a distribution over latent codes and a
> decoder that maps codes back to data, trained by maximizing the ELBO. The reparameterization
> trick makes the sampling step differentiable. VAEs give you a structured latent space and fast
> one-shot sampling at the cost of blurry outputs — which is why they survive today not as
> standalone generators but as the **compressor inside latent diffusion** and as the **tokenizer**
> (VQ-VAE) in front of autoregressive image, audio and video models.

**Prerequisites**: → [Probability & information theory](../01-foundations/02-probability-and-information-theory.md) · **Next**: → [GAN](03-gan.md)

---

## 1. The setup

Assume data is generated by a two-step process:

$$z \sim p(z) = \mathcal{N}(0, I), \qquad x \sim p_\theta(x \mid z)$$

$z$ is a low-dimensional **latent code** (the "concept"), $x$ the observed data (the "rendering").
To train by maximum likelihood we need

$$p_\theta(x) = \int p_\theta(x\mid z)p(z)\,dz$$

which is intractable — it integrates a neural network over a $d_z$-dimensional space.

> [!WARNING]
> **Why not Monte Carlo?** $p_\theta(x) \approx \frac{1}{K}\sum_k p_\theta(x \mid z_k)$ with
> $z_k \sim p(z)$. In high dimensions almost every random $z$ decodes to something nothing like $x$,
> so almost every term is ≈ 0. You'd need astronomically many samples. **The fix is importance
> sampling from a learned proposal $q_\phi(z\mid x)$** — which is exactly the encoder.

---

## 2. The ELBO, and what each half wants

$$\log p_\theta(x) = \underbrace{\mathcal{L}(\theta,\phi;x)}_{\text{ELBO}} + \underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\phi(z|x)\,\|\,p_\theta(z|x))}_{\ge 0}$$

(full derivation: → [Probability & information theory §7](../01-foundations/02-probability-and-information-theory.md#7-the-elbo-what-to-do-when-the-likelihood-is-intractable))

$$\boxed{\;\mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}\!\left[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)\right]}_{\text{reconstruction}} - \underbrace{D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(q_\phi(z\mid x)\,\|\,p(z)\right)}_{\text{rate / regularizer}}\;}$$

```
                              ENCODER                    DECODER
                         q_φ(z|x) = N(μ,σ²)           p_θ(x|z)
                    ┌──────────────────────┐      ┌────────────────┐
   x ──────────────►│  conv/MLP  ──► μ(x)  │      │                │
   (784-dim)        │            ──► σ(x)  │      │  deconv/MLP    │──► x̂
                    └──────────┬───────────┘      └───────▲────────┘
                               │                          │
                               │   z = μ + σ⊙ε            │
                               └──────────►(+)────────────┘
                                            ▲
                                       ε ~ N(0,I)
                                    (reparameterization)

   LOSS = ‖x − x̂‖²           +      β · KL(N(μ,σ²) ‖ N(0,I))
          "be accurate"              "be close to the prior"
          ← pulls σ→0, μ spread      → pushes σ→1, μ→0
```

> [!TIP]
> **The tension, stated plainly** — the reconstruction term wants each input to get its own
> private, tightly-concentrated region of latent space ($\sigma \to 0$, codes far apart): that makes
> decoding unambiguous. The KL term wants every input to map to the *same* standard Gaussian
> ($\mu \to 0, \sigma \to 1$): that makes the latent space smooth and samplable. The equilibrium is a
> latent space where codes overlap *just* enough that sampling $z \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I)$ lands
> somewhere the decoder understands.

**Without the KL term you have a plain autoencoder**: great reconstruction, but the latent space is
full of holes. Sample a random $z$ and you decode garbage. The KL term is what makes it
*generative*.

### The rate–distortion reading

$$-\mathcal{L} = \underbrace{D}_{\text{distortion}} + \underbrace{R}_{\text{rate}}$$

The KL term is literally the **rate**: the expected number of nats needed to transmit $z$ under a
code optimized for the prior. The reconstruction term is the **distortion**. A VAE is a learned
lossy compressor, and $\beta$ (below) slides you along the rate–distortion curve.

## 3. The reparameterization trick

We need $\nabla_\phi \mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z|x)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)]$, but the distribution being
sampled from depends on $\phi$. Sampling is not differentiable.

**Solution**: move the randomness to a parameter-free source.

$$z = \mu_\phi(x) + \sigma_\phi(x)\odot\epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$$

Now $z$ is a *deterministic, differentiable* function of $\phi$ and a *fixed* random variable.

```
   ✗ BLOCKED                          ✓ FLOWS
   
   φ ──► (μ,σ) ──► [SAMPLE] ──► z     φ ──► (μ,σ) ──┐
                       ▲                             ├──► z = μ+σ⊙ε
              stochastic node;                ε ─────┘
              no gradient path                (constant w.r.t. φ)
```

**Why it's correct** — it is a change of variables inside the expectation:

$$\mathbb{E}_{z\sim q_\phi}[f(z)] = \mathbb{E}_{\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)}\big[f(\mu_\phi + \sigma_\phi\odot\epsilon)\big]$$

The right-hand side's distribution no longer depends on $\phi$, so
$\nabla_\phi$ passes straight inside.

**Variance**: the reparameterized estimator typically has variance orders of magnitude below
REINFORCE, which is the difference between "trains in an hour" and "does not train."

The standard implementation detail: the encoder outputs $\log\sigma^2$, not $\sigma$, so that the
output is unconstrained (any real number) and $\sigma = \exp(\frac12\log\sigma^2) > 0$ automatically.

```python
mu, logvar = self.encoder(x).chunk(2, dim=-1)
std = torch.exp(0.5 * logvar)
z = mu + std * torch.randn_like(std)                       # reparameterize
kl = -0.5 * torch.sum(1 + logvar - mu.pow(2) - logvar.exp(), dim=1)
```

## 4. The loss, concretely

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{VAE}} = \underbrace{\|x - \hat x\|_2^2}_{\text{Gaussian decoder}}
\;+\; \underbrace{\tfrac12\sum_{j=1}^{d_z}\left(\mu_j^2 + \sigma_j^2 - \log\sigma_j^2 - 1\right)}_{\text{KL to } \mathcal{N}(0,I)}$$

The choice of reconstruction term **is** a choice of decoder likelihood:

| Decoder likelihood | Reconstruction loss | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| $\mathcal{N}(\hat x, \sigma^2 I)$, fixed $\sigma$ | MSE | continuous data (default) |
| Bernoulli | binary cross-entropy | binarized MNIST |
| Categorical (256-way per channel) | cross-entropy | discretized pixels |
| Laplace | L1 | robustness to outliers |
| + LPIPS + adversarial | perceptual + GAN | **latent diffusion VAEs** |

> [!WARNING]
> **This is the blurriness, explained.** With a Gaussian likelihood, the optimal decoder output is
> $\mathbb{E}[x \mid z]$ — the *average* of all images consistent with $z$. If $z$ doesn't pin down
> the exact location of an edge, the average of "edge at pixel 10" and "edge at pixel 11" is a
> smeared edge at 10.5. MSE literally rewards hedging.

```
   Two plausible reconstructions      MSE-optimal output = their mean
      ██▓▓░░              ░░▓▓██              ▓▓▒▒▒▒▓▓
      sharp, at left     sharp, at right     blurry, in between
                                             ← lower MSE than either!
```

> [!TIP]
> **The fixes, in increasing order of effectiveness:**
> 1. Use a richer likelihood (discretized logistic mixture) — helps a little.
> 2. Use a **perceptual loss** (LPIPS: distance in a pretrained VGG's feature space) — helps a lot,
>    because feature-space averaging is not pixel-space averaging.
> 3. Add an **adversarial loss** on the decoder — a discriminator punishes blur directly. This is
>    what the Stable Diffusion VAE does, and it is why its reconstructions are sharp.
> 4. Use **discrete latents** (VQ-VAE) + an expressive autoregressive prior — moves the
>    "which of the plausible images" decision to a model that can represent multimodality.

## 5. The two classic failure modes

### Posterior collapse

The KL term is minimized at $q_\phi(z\mid x) = p(z)$ — i.e. the encoder ignores $x$ entirely. If the
decoder is powerful enough to model $p(x)$ on its own (e.g. an autoregressive decoder), this is a
*perfectly good local optimum*: KL = 0, reconstruction handled by the decoder alone.

Result: $I(x; z) = 0$. The latent is dead. You have trained an expensive autoregressive model with
a useless noise input.

| Fix | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **KL annealing** | ramp $\beta$ from 0 → 1 over training; let reconstruction establish a useful code first |
| **Free bits** | $\max(\lambda, \text{KL}_j)$ per dimension — no gradient pressure below $\lambda$ nats |
| Weaken the decoder | limit capacity / receptive field so it *needs* $z$ |
| Skip connections from $z$ | inject $z$ at every decoder layer |
| **Discrete latents (VQ)** | no KL term to collapse at all |

Free bits, the most robust fix:

```python
kl_per_dim = -0.5 * (1 + logvar - mu.pow(2) - logvar.exp())   # (B, d_z)
kl = torch.clamp(kl_per_dim, min=free_bits).sum(dim=1).mean() # λ ≈ 0.03–0.5 nats/dim
```

### Holes in the prior (the aggregate posterior mismatch)

The KL term only constrains $q_\phi(z\mid x)$ **per example**. What matters for sampling is the
*aggregate* posterior $q_\phi(z) = \mathbb{E}_{x}[q_\phi(z\mid x)]$. These can differ a lot.

```
   Prior p(z) = N(0,I)         Aggregate posterior q(z)
       ╭───────────╮               ╭───────────╮
       │  ░░░░░░░  │               │ ██   ░  ██│   ← clusters + gaps
       │ ░░░░░░░░░ │      vs       │   ██   ██ │
       │  ░░░░░░░  │               │ ██  ░░  █ │
       ╰───────────╯               ╰───────────╯
    sample from here          but the decoder only knows here
                              ⇒ samples from the gaps decode to garbage
```

**Fixes**: a learned prior (VampPrior, a flow, or — most effectively — **a second generative model
trained on the latents**). That last option is precisely latent diffusion: train a VAE, then train
a diffusion model on $q_\phi(z)$ instead of assuming it is Gaussian.
→ [Latent diffusion](../05-diffusion-and-vision/03-latent-diffusion.md)

## 6. Beta-VAE and disentanglement

$$\mathcal{L}_\beta = \mathbb{E}_{q}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)] - \beta\, D_{\mathrm{KL}}(q_\phi(z|x)\|p(z))$$

| $\beta$ | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| $\beta = 0$ | plain autoencoder: sharp reconstructions, unsamplable latent |
| $\beta = 1$ | the true ELBO |
| $\beta > 1$ | more disentangled, more compressed, blurrier |
| $\beta \to \infty$ | posterior collapse |

> [!TIP]
> **Why large $\beta$ encourages disentanglement** — a large KL penalty forces the model through a
> narrow information channel. To reconstruct well under a tight bit budget, the encoder must use the
> *most efficient* code, and for data generated by independent factors (pose, lighting, identity)
> the most efficient code is one that allocates a separate dimension to each factor. The isotropic
> Gaussian prior additionally prefers axis-aligned, independent dimensions.

> [!WARNING]
> **The honest caveat** — Locatello et al. (2019), *Challenging Common Assumptions in the
> Unsupervised Learning of Disentangled Representations*, proved that unsupervised disentanglement is
> **impossible without inductive biases**, and showed empirically that reported gains were largely
> attributable to random seed variation and unprincipled model selection. Treat disentanglement
> claims sceptically; if you need disentangled factors, use some supervision.

## 7. VQ-VAE: discrete latents

Replace the continuous Gaussian latent with a lookup into a learned **codebook**
$\mathcal{E} = \{e_1,\dots,e_K\} \subset \mathbb{R}^{d}$.

$$z_q(x) = e_k, \qquad k = \arg\min_j \|z_e(x) - e_j\|_2$$

```
  x ──►[encoder]──► z_e ──► ┌─────────────┐ ──► z_q ──►[decoder]──► x̂
   (H,W,3)        (h,w,d)   │  CODEBOOK   │   (h,w,d)
                            │ e₁ e₂ … e_K │
                            └─────────────┘
                     nearest-neighbour lookup
                     output is a grid of INTEGERS (h,w) ∈ {1..K}
```

**The loss** (three terms):

$$\mathcal{L} = \underbrace{\|x - D(z_q)\|_2^2}_{\text{reconstruction}}
+ \underbrace{\|\mathrm{sg}[z_e] - e\|_2^2}_{\text{codebook}}
+ \beta\underbrace{\|z_e - \mathrm{sg}[e]\|_2^2}_{\text{commitment}}$$

where $\mathrm{sg}[\cdot]$ is stop-gradient. Term 2 moves codebook vectors toward encoder outputs;
term 3 stops the encoder from drifting away from the codebook ($\beta \approx 0.25$).

**Straight-through estimator** — $\arg\min$ has zero gradient everywhere, so copy the gradient
across the quantization boundary:

```python
z_q = z_e + (z_q - z_e).detach()   # forward: z_q. backward: gradient flows to z_e.
```

**The compression numbers** — a $256\times256\times3$ image → a $32\times32$ grid of indices
into a 512-entry codebook:

$$\frac{32 \times 32 \times \log_2 512}{256\times256\times3\times8} = \frac{9216 \text{ bits}}{1{,}572{,}864 \text{ bits}} = \frac{1}{170}$$

**A 170× compression into 1024 discrete tokens.** Now run a Transformer over those 1024 tokens —
an entirely tractable sequence length. That two-stage recipe is DALL·E 1, VQGAN, Parti, MusicLM,
and most video generation.

| Model | What VQ gave it |
|---|---|
| VQ-VAE-2 | hierarchical codes → high-res images with a PixelCNN prior |
| VQGAN | + adversarial and perceptual loss → sharp, low token count |
| DALL·E 1 | text tokens + image tokens in one autoregressive stream |
| EnCodec / SoundStream | audio → tokens → language model over audio |
| Modern video models | spatio-temporal VQ + Transformer |

> [!WARNING]
> **Codebook collapse** — most entries go unused; effective vocabulary shrinks to a handful.
> Fixes: EMA codebook updates, restarting dead codes to random encoder outputs, low-dimensional
> codes with L2 normalization, or **FSQ (finite scalar quantization)**, which replaces the learned
> codebook with fixed per-dimension rounding and sidesteps the problem entirely.

## 8. Implementation

A complete VAE on MNIST, end to end:

```python
import torch, torch.nn as nn, torch.nn.functional as F

class VAE(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, x_dim=784, h=512, z_dim=20):
        super().__init__()
        self.enc = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(x_dim, h), nn.ReLU(),
                                 nn.Linear(h, h), nn.ReLU(),
                                 nn.Linear(h, 2 * z_dim))       # -> mu, logvar
        self.dec = nn.Sequential(nn.Linear(z_dim, h), nn.ReLU(),
                                 nn.Linear(h, h), nn.ReLU(),
                                 nn.Linear(h, x_dim))           # -> logits

    def forward(self, x):
        mu, logvar = self.enc(x).chunk(2, dim=-1)
        logvar = logvar.clamp(-8, 8)                 # numerical safety
        z = mu + torch.exp(0.5 * logvar) * torch.randn_like(mu)
        return self.dec(z), mu, logvar

def loss_fn(x_logits, x, mu, logvar, beta=1.0):
    # Bernoulli likelihood: sum over pixels, mean over batch
    recon = F.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits(x_logits, x, reduction='none').sum(1).mean()
    kl    = (-0.5 * (1 + logvar - mu.pow(2) - logvar.exp())).sum(1).mean()
    return recon + beta * kl, recon, kl

# training loop
model, opt = VAE(), None
opt = torch.optim.Adam(model.parameters(), lr=1e-3)
for epoch in range(20):
    for x, _ in loader:
        x = x.view(x.size(0), -1)
        x_logits, mu, logvar = model(x)
        # KL annealing over the first 10 epochs guards against collapse
        beta = min(1.0, epoch / 10)
        loss, recon, kl = loss_fn(x_logits, x, mu, logvar, beta)
        opt.zero_grad(); loss.backward(); opt.step()

# sampling: no encoder involved
with torch.no_grad():
    z = torch.randn(64, 20)
    samples = torch.sigmoid(model.dec(z)).view(-1, 1, 28, 28)
```

**Expected numbers on binarized MNIST**, $d_z = 20$, after ~20 epochs:
reconstruction ≈ 70 nats, KL ≈ 25 nats, total ≈ 95 nats/image. Reported state-of-the-art ELBOs are
around 80 nats; PixelCNN-decoder VAEs reach ~78.

**Sanity check on the KL value**: 25 nats = 36 bits of information in the latent code. For MNIST
that is plausible — digit identity (3.3 bits) plus ~30 bits of style. If your KL is near 0, you
have posterior collapse. If it is near the reconstruction loss, $\beta$ is too low.

## 9. Where VAEs actually live today

| Role | System | Why a VAE |
|---|---|---|
| **Latent compressor** | Stable Diffusion, SDXL, Flux | 8× spatial downsample → 64× fewer pixels to diffuse. This is the single most important VAE deployment. |
| **Tokenizer** | DALL·E 1, VQGAN, Parti, video models | discrete codes → a Transformer can model images/video |
| **Audio codec** | EnCodec, SoundStream, DAC | residual VQ → audio as a token stream |
| **Molecule design** | JT-VAE, chemical VAEs | continuous latent enables gradient-based optimization of properties |
| **Anomaly detection** | industrial, medical | high reconstruction error = out of distribution |
| **World models** | Dreamer, model-based RL | compact latent state for planning |

> [!TIP]
> **The lesson worth generalizing** — VAEs lost as *generators* but won as *representation
> learners*. Splitting a hard generative problem into "compress to a good space" + "generate in that
> space" is one of the most reusable architectural patterns in the field.

## 10. Key takeaways

| # | Takeaway |
|---|---|
| 1 | ELBO = reconstruction − KL. The two terms fight; that fight is the entire model. |
| 2 | Reparameterization ($z = \mu + \sigma\epsilon$) is what makes the gradient flow. |
| 3 | Blurriness comes from the Gaussian likelihood: MSE's optimum is the *mean* of plausible outputs. |
| 4 | Fix blur with perceptual + adversarial decoder losses (what latent-diffusion VAEs do). |
| 5 | Posterior collapse = KL → 0 = dead latent. Fix with annealing or free bits. |
| 6 | The prior/aggregate-posterior gap causes bad samples; fix it by *learning* the latent distribution. |
| 7 | VQ-VAE gives discrete tokens → unlocks Transformers over images, audio and video. ~170× compression. |
| 8 | Disentanglement from $\beta$ alone is not reliably achievable without inductive bias or supervision. |
